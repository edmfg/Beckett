## 2. The Shared Mechanic (Explainer Content)

This is the "how it works" the site must teach before any mockup makes sense.

- A big sports moment isn't just attention — it's a **question generator**.
- **NBCU ACR/OCR** "reads" the live broadcast and detects game-state.
- That signal **triggers a contextual CTV ad** on the same screen, in the moment.
- The ad hands the viewer off to **Google Search (AI Mode)** — the place the
  question gets a real, reasoned answer.
- Old Search: type keywords → scan ten links → assemble the answer yourself.
- AI Mode: ask it like the friend on the couch → answered before the replay ends.

Explain it as a 4-step flow: **Signal → Trigger → Ad → Search.**

## 3. CONCEPT 1 — TOO CLOSE TO CALL

### 3.1 Lockup
- **Concept name:** TOO CLOSE TO CALL
- **Step-up line:** "Too close to call? Call it."
- **Endline:** Search on.
- The booth throws up its hands. You don't have to. The broadcast can't call
  it — AI Mode can.

### 3.2 The Idea
A close game turns your living room into an argument. Google settles it.
The CTV ad drops a *take* into the room mid-argument and walks away — trusting
the fan to grab their phone and check if it's right. AI Mode is the payoff:
it has a defensible, reasoned answer.

The ad is the **heckler at the bar**, not the professor at the front. Nobody
fact-checks a professor. Everybody fact-checks a loud heckler.

### 3.3 Why It Works in Both Sports
The concept spans **college football and the NBA** — but the *arguments have
different shapes*, and the creative respects that:

- **College football** — the late-game question is **strategy**: punt or go
  for it, play for the tie or the win. Plus Playoff/ranking stakes layered on
  top. Tribal identity (alma mater, region) makes every take personal.
- **NBA** — the late-game question is **nerve**: who takes the shot, who
  disappears, shot selection, clutch. The argument is about *players and guts*,
  not playbook.

> Don't write NBA takes as football takes with a ball swapped in. Football =
> "what should they DO." Basketball = "who has the NERVE."

Analogy: a close football game is a *chess endgame* — it's about the right
move. A close basketball game is a *high-noon duel* — it's about who blinks.

### 3.4 The Trigger
The NBCU signal reliably knows two things — all the concept needs:
1. **The game is close** (within ~7 pts football / ~6 pts NBA).
2. **It's late** (halftime, or 4th quarter).

Trigger logic = smart and selective. Creative copy = evergreen, dumb-on-purpose.
The intelligence is in *when* it fires, not *what it says*.

Game-state beats single-play: a play is a 25-second latency sprint; a close
score is a sustained condition with a comfortable window. Blowouts filter
themselves out — nobody grabs a phone down 30.

### 3.5 The Creative System — "Drop a Take and Walk Away"
The CTV spot states a provocative, evergreen take, then steps up:
**"Too close to call? Call it." → Search on.**

Rules:
- Aimed at the **situation**, never the **fan's team**.
- No team names, no score, no player names → runs all season untouched.
- Strategic accusations are strongest — debatable, not personal.

**Hero take (CFB):** "The wrong guy has the ball right now."
**Hero take (NBA):** "Somebody on this floor just disappeared."

Take bank — College Football:
- "That punt was a coward's punt."
- "Your QB is playing scared."
- "This game was lost in the 2nd quarter."
- "You're blaming the defense. It's not the defense."
- "A close win over an unranked team is not a statement."
- "This result just blew up the rankings."

Take bank — NBA:
- "Somebody on this floor just disappeared."
- "Your star hasn't taken a real shot in 8 minutes."
- "That timeout came too late."
- "This isn't a close game. One team is choking."
- "The bench is the reason you're still in this."
- "Wrong guy is about to take the last shot."

### 3.6 The Search Prompts (AI Mode)
Middle zone: specific enough to be a real question, evergreen enough to run
all season. Tell of a good one — fan reads it and thinks "...huh, I don't
actually know."

**Hero prompt (cross-sport):** "Is it better to have the ball or the lead late
in a close game?"

College Football:
- "Is it smarter to punt or go for it on 4th down late in a close game"
- "Should a team play for the tie or the win when down 6 in the 4th"
- "Does a close win hurt a team's Playoff chances"
- "How much do the rankings move after a close upset"

NBA:
- "Should you foul up 3 in the final seconds"
- "Is it better to take the last shot or play for two possessions"
- "Who usually decides a close NBA game — the star or the role players"
- "Why do teams go cold in the 4th quarter of close games"
- "How important is the bench in a tight playoff game"

### 3.7 Scope
- **Sports:** College Football **and** NBA.
- **Window:** effectively year-round across the two — CFB late Aug → CFP final
  in January; NBA's clutch stretch runs deep winter → the June Finals.
- **Out of scope:** NFL, and the World Cup final (Jul 19, 2026 — different
  broadcast/deal). Don't let the pitch get pulled into other sports.

## 4. THE ATHLETE LAYER (Google-Sponsored Talent)

Google-sponsored athletes are available to feature. The rule: **athletes never
go inside the in-game CTV takes** — that would break the evergreen engine (a
named athlete is specific and dated; the whole system depends on no names).
Instead, athletes live in a separate **hero-film / brand layer** that wraps the
system.

### 4.1 Relevance screen
**Fits this concept (NBA only):**
- **Jimmy Butler** — strongest fit. "Playoff Jimmy" *is* a clutch-nerve
  narrative; he embodies the NBA take's exact shape ("who has the nerve").
- **Steph Curry** — the clutch-shooting argument personified. The right answer
  to "wrong guy about to take the last shot."
- **Giannis Antetokounmpo** — fits, but he's a force-of-nature argument, not a
  nerve argument. Use sparingly; weaker fit for a "who blinks" concept.
- **A'ja Wilson** — strong clutch/"who takes over" fit, BUT requires widening
  scope to the **WNBA**. Treat as an *optional expansion*, not baked in.

**Does NOT fit — explicitly parked so nobody forces them later:**
- **Trinity Rodman** — soccer; out of scope.
- **Lando Norris, Oscar Piastri, Bianca Bustamante** — F1/motorsport; no
  "close game, 4th quarter" couch-argument structure.
- **Bryson DeChambeau** — golf; no late-game-score trigger.

> Note: **College Football has zero athletes on the sponsored list.** The CFB
> half of the concept runs faceless. The athlete play is **NBA-only.**

### 4.2 How athletes are used (without breaking evergreen)
- **Voice of the heckler** — an athlete delivers the takes in a launch/hero
  spot. Butler saying "Somebody on this floor just disappeared" to camera works
  — he's earned the right to call it. The *system* CTV ads stay nameless; the
  *brand* film has a face.
- **The bookend** — athlete opens or closes the campaign film; the evergreen
  in-game CTV ads do the real-time work.
- **Settling it themselves** — meta spot: the athlete is the one who pulls out
  a phone and searches. Steph checking "is it better to take the last shot or
  play for two" — the guy who'd know, still asking. Charming, on-message.

### 4.3 Build implication
- The 10 in-game CTV mockups in Section 5 stay **nameless** — do not add
  athletes to them.
- Add a small **"Athlete Layer" callout** on the Concept 1 page explaining the
  hero-film role, featuring Butler + Curry. Keep it visually separate from the
  in-game mockup gallery so the evergreen logic stays clear.

## 6. Design Direction — STADIUM TUNNEL (locked)

Theme selected: **Stadium Tunnel.**

- **Mood:** high-contrast, bold, motion-forward — the energy of walking out of
  the tunnel into a packed stadium/arena. Matches the passion of both sports.
- **Type:** large, confident display headlines; tight, punchy body. Big type
  is the hero — let it dominate.
- **Color:** dark base, dramatic high contrast, one decisive accent per
  concept. TOO CLOSE TO CALL accent = broadcast-red or "too close" amber.
- **Layout:** scroll-driven sequence — the site reveals as a confident march,
  section by section, like emerging from the tunnel toward the field/court.
- **Motion:** staggered reveals on scroll, high-impact entrances; restrained
  enough to still read as *thinking*, not a hype reel.
- **CTV mockup component:** literal thin TV bezel; the Visualize caption/
  illustration sits beside or below each mockup. CFB and NBA mockups can share
  the component — just swap the on-screen content.

> Gentle flag: Stadium Tunnel's risk is style overpowering substance. The
> mechanic explainer (Section 2) must stay clear and legible — let the bold
> energy live in the concept reveals, keep the "how it works" calm.

## 7. Open Risks / To Pressure-Test

- **Latency:** copy is state-based, so safe. Confirm NBCU per-moment serve time
  before any future play-level triggers.
- **The prediction scoreboard:** if the ad implies a *winner* and the game
  flips, that's visible. Frame AI Mode as "here's how to think about it," not
  "here's the winner." Sell the reasoning, not the verdict.
- **Generic risk:** evergreen copy can read as wallpaper — saved by voice and
  timing (the ad must feel like it's eavesdropping on the room).
- **Don't punch the viewer:** every take aims at the situation, never the team.
- **Cross-sport drift:** keep football takes about strategy, NBA takes about
  nerve. If they start sounding interchangeable, the writing has gone generic.
- **Athlete contamination:** never let a named athlete into the in-game CTV
  takes — it breaks the evergreen engine. Athletes stay in the brand layer.
