Concept 01 — Google Search · AI Mode × NBCU CTV

Too Close To Call

The booth can't call it. The room won't let it go. That's the opening — and Google takes it.

STEP-UP · "Too close to call? Call it." ENDLINE · Search on. SPORTS · CFB + NBA
↓ Scroll — Run it back
01

When it's close,
the couch gets loud.

A close game turns your living room into an argument. Google settles it. The CTV ad drops a take into the room mid-argument and walks away — trusting the fan to grab their phone and check if it's right. AI Mode is the payoff: it has a defensible, reasoned answer.

The ad is the heckler at the bar, not the professor at the front. Nobody fact-checks a professor. Everybody fact-checks a loud heckler.

02

Same shape.
Different argument.

The concept spans college football and the NBA — but the arguments have different shapes, and the creative respects that. Don't write NBA takes as football takes with a ball swapped in.

College Football
is a chess endgame.

The late-game question is strategy: punt or go for it, play for the tie or the win. Plus Playoff/ranking stakes layered on top.

Tribal identity — alma mater, region — makes every take personal. It's about the right move.

FOOTBALL = WHAT SHOULD THEY DO.

The NBA
is a high-noon duel.

The late-game question is nerve: who takes the shot, who disappears, shot selection, clutch. The argument is about players and guts, not playbook.

It's about who blinks.

BASKETBALL = WHO HAS THE NERVE.
03

The signal reads
the game-state.

NBCU's ACR/OCR feed waits for two things to be true. No play-by-play sprint — a sustained state. When both light up, the CTV ad serves on the same screen.

Thresholds: ~7 points CFB / ~6 points NBA. Window: halftime or 4th quarter. Trigger logic is smart and selective. Creative copy is evergreen, dumb-on-purpose. The intelligence is in when it fires, not what it says.

04

Drop a take.
Walk away.

One evergreen provocation, aimed at the situation — never the fan's team. They disagree, or they wonder. Either way, the thumb is moving. The lockup steps up: "Too close to call? Call it." → Search on.

1
Aimed at the situation
Never the fan's team. Strategic accusations are strongest — debatable, not personal.
2
No names, no scores
No team names, no players, no score numbers. Runs all season untouched. The engine stays evergreen.
3
Eavesdrop, don't lecture
The ad sounds like something someone in the room already said. Saved by voice and timing, not by writing harder.

07

The athlete layer
(NBA only).

A hero / brand-film layer that wraps the system

Named athletes never go inside the in-game CTV takes — that would break the evergreen engine. They live in a separate brand layer: hero spots, bookends, meta moments where the athlete is the one searching. CFB runs faceless.

Strongest fit
Jimmy
Butler
Voice of the heckler
"Playoff Jimmy" is a clutch-nerve narrative — he embodies the NBA take's exact shape ("who has the nerve"). Butler saying "Somebody on this floor just disappeared" to camera works — he's earned the right to call it.
Hero fit
Steph
Curry
The one settling it
The clutch-shooting argument personified — the right answer to "wrong guy about to take the last shot." Meta spot: Steph pulls out a phone and searches "is it better to take the last shot or play for two." The guy who'd know, still asking. Charming, on-message.
The in-game CTV ads stay nameless. Always. Athletes wrap the system in the brand layer — never inside it.
08

Open risks.
Pressure-test.

The places this can go wrong, named on purpose so nobody has to discover them at the wrong moment.

Latency
Copy is state-based, so safe. Confirm NBCU per-moment serve time before any future play-level triggers.
Prediction scoreboard
Never imply a winner. Frame AI Mode as "here's how to think about it," not "here's the answer." Sell the reasoning.
Generic risk
Evergreen copy can read as wallpaper — saved by voice and timing. The ad must feel like it's eavesdropping on the room.
Don't punch the viewer
Every take aims at the situation, never the team. Strategic accusations, not personal ones.
Cross-sport drift
Football = strategy. NBA = nerve. If they start sounding interchangeable, the writing has gone generic.
Athlete contamination
No named athlete in the in-game CTV takes — it breaks the evergreen engine. Athletes stay in the brand layer.
